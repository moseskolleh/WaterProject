# Repair roadmap

This is the list of what is wrong with the toolkit's outputs, in the order
it will be fixed. It was built from an audit of the three worked examples
(`examples/projects/`) as regenerated by the current code: every figure was
looked at, every `.docx` was dumped and read as a client or a ministry
reviewer would read it, and every number was traced back to the code that
produced it. Eight areas were audited by independent auditors, and every
finding below was reproduced by the auditor that reported it, with a
script or by dumping the figure or document. A few findings carry an
independent second verification or a direct check by the maintainer of
this branch, and are marked as such.

Severity is about consequence, not effort:

- **C** critical: a wrong result reaches a client document or a drilling
  decision.
- **H** high: a misleading output, or two outputs of one project that
  contradict each other.
- **M** medium: a degraded or unclear output.
- **L** low: cosmetic.

Status marks: `[ ]` open, `[x]` fixed on this branch. "verified" means an
independent second auditor tried to refute the finding and could not;
"checked" means it was reproduced a second time by hand on this branch.

## Execution order

The order is by consequence first, then by dependency, then by locality:
what feeds a drilling decision goes before what describes it; the rule a
figure depends on goes before the figure; fixes in one file go together.

0. Housekeeping: orphaned example outputs, and a guard so stale outputs fail CI.
1. Depth of investigation and VES interpretation. Feeds siting, the sections, the geophysical report and the pre-drilling design.
2. Survey-scale maps and sections. The report crashes on a straight traverse; the section, the captions and the drill-target map are wrong.
3. Pumping-test hydraulics. The transmissivity, the pump depth and the "sustainable" verdict.
4. Borehole design and drawing. Screens against the wrong depths, a design captioned as-built, a pack that cannot be placed.
5. Report text and documents. Boilerplate, captions, claims and tables; the water-quality verdicts.
6. Regional cartography and bundled data. The maps at the front of every report.
7. Browser engine and Streamlit parity. Every step above carries its mirror; this step is what only the browser gets wrong.
8. Data, geodesy and parsers. Two critical parser defects (non-detects read
   as measurements; an unsigned longitude placed 250 km away with no flag)
   and the hole rings that erase dykes from the maps; ordered last only
   because the engines above must be fixed before their inputs are
   re-plumbed, and each of these is a self-contained fix that can be
   pulled forward.

Every engine change lands as Python + JavaScript mirror + tests +
regenerated example outputs + parity reference, in one commit, so the
two engines never disagree on a client document (see "Cross-cutting").

## 1. Depth of investigation and VES interpretation

Files: `ves/interpret.py`, `ves/inversion.py`, `ves/splice.py`,

- [x] **C ves-1. A conductive half-space is reported as a water zone down
  to max AB/2, and the drilling depth is the array length.** Both Rokel
  soundings end in a 47-50 ohm-m half-space below 8 m; the report says
  "water bearing zone 8 m to 80 m", "aquifer thickness 72 m", "drill to
  80 m", where 80 m is simply the largest electrode half-spacing. A
  Schlumberger sounding resolves roughly AB/2 divided by 2 to 3, and a
  half-space has no base at all. checked.
- [x] **C ves-2. Any conductive bottom layer is labelled "fractured bedrock,
  low resistivity indicative of groundwater in fractures".** Under a
  laterite cap on the Freetown gabbro a 47 ohm-m unit persisting to the
  depth of investigation is the clay-rich weathered zone whose base was not
  reached; fractured fresh gabbro is hundreds to thousands of ohm-m.
- [x] **H ves-3. The ranking prefers B (2) over A (1) on 2.7 ohm-m of
  unresolved half-space resistivity while its fit is twice as bad.** Both
  score 68; the tie is an artefact of rounding (68.0 vs 67.6); nothing in
  the score uses the misfit, convergence or whether basement was resolved;
  two scoring systems exist (interpret.score and assess_siting).
- [x] **H ves-4. A misfit above the 10 percent target is never flagged.**
  A (1) fits at 13.3 percent and B (2) at 26.8 percent; neither reaches the
  documented target, so the model is chosen by an undocumented "within
  15 percent of the best" fallback, and the alternative models tried
  (a 3-layer B (2) with basement at 53 m) are never shown.
- [x] **H ves-5. Overlapping readings that disagree by 45-98 percent at the
  MN changes are merged by geometric mean and flagged only as INFO.** The
  spliced curve kinks and the misfit is partly manufactured by the splice;
  the field problem is invisible to the geophysicist. Done: a WARNING
  names each pair and its ratio. The splice itself still merges by
  geometric mean; a mode that drops or down-weights the outlying reading
  is a data-treatment decision left to the geophysicist the warning now
  reaches.
- [x] **M ves-6. "Resolves a 3 layer subsurface" when the first boundary is
  x/3.7 unresolved** and a refit without it costs two points of a 13
  percent misfit. Done: the sounding block names any boundary with a
  thickness factor of 2 or more as poorly resolved.
- [x] **M ves-7. The original IPI2Win models are imported and discarded.**
  No comparison reaches the report, and the toolkit cannot reproduce their
  reported ERR (35.8 vs 21.5 percent, 96.7 vs 26.5 percent). Done in the
  Python report (the browser has no IPI2Win reader).
- [x] **M ves-8 / maps-survey-4. Four different depth-of-investigation
  rules.** Max AB/2 in the interpretation, half of it in the layer column,
  0.35-0.7 in the starting models, "the maximum electrode spacing" in the
  limitations text; the model panel of the curve figure runs to 80 m and the
  layer column beside it to 40 m. Done for the interpretation and every
  figure; the starting models' 0.35-0.7 bracket is a search heuristic, not
  a claim, and stays.
- [x] **M ves-9 / reports-12. The preference table column "Apparent
  Resistivity (Ohm-m)" holds the inverted layer resistivities.**

Steps: one `VESConfig.depth_of_investigation_factor` (default 0.5 of max
AB/2, referenced) used by the interpretation, every figure, the starting
models and the limitations text; an open-ended zone is `(top, DOI)` with a
`basement_not_resolved` flag, the aquifer thickness a lower bound, and the
maximum drilling depth from resolved geology capped at the DOI; the bottom
conductive layer is "weathered zone, base not resolved"; a `poor_fit` flag
when ERR exceeds the target, propagated to the sounding block, the summary
and the recommendation; an overlap-discrepancy WARNING with the ratio; a
confidence term in the suitability score and a tie said in words; one
scoring system; the trials table and the reference model beside the
toolkit's in the report; rename the column.

## Cross-cutting rules for every fix

- Python and JavaScript change in the same commit; then
  `python tests/webapp/make_reference.py`, `node tests/webapp/parity.mjs`,
  `python web/build_webapp_data.py` if data changed, and
  `python web/build_offline.py` last.
- A figure change changes its caption; the three examples are regenerated
  and their inputs and outputs are committed together; `build_catalogue.py`
  is regenerated in the same change.
- Flags reach the client documents: design flags, pumping-test flags and
  VES flags each get a "checks" block in the report that carries them.
- One depth-of-investigation rule, one rounding rule, one lithology class
  table, one UTM-zone rule, shared by both engines and pinned in
  `reference.json`.
- Prose that both engines write is worded once and held to parity; prose
  only one engine writes is asserted in `review.mjs`.
