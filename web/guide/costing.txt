distributionThe bill of quantities is the contract document: it prices one borehole at the likely
rate of every item, and it is what a contractor prices and is paid against. The distribution is the
planning figure: what a borehole is likely to cost once the spread of every rate, the drilling depth
and the chance of a dry hole are allowed for. Budget from the planning figure; contract on the bill
of quantities.A completed borehole costs US$ 10,642 at P50 and US$ 10,959 at P80, over 20,000
sampled boreholes. The bill of quantities prices it at US$ 10,135, at or above the cost of 6 percent
of them.Each figure is the contract price with any VAT, before contingency, worked out from the
direct cost with the same overheads (15 percent) and margin (20 percent) as the bill of quantities;
the spread takes the place of the contingency. With its 10 percent contingency the planning budget,
US$ 11,148, covers 91 percent of the sampled boreholes.No survey has been interpreted, so no dry
hole is drawn, and the expected cost per working borehole is the mean, US$ 10,657; interpret the
survey to allow for dry holes at its odds.The range of models has not been sampled, so the depth is
held at 70 m and its spread is left out; sample the range of models to draw the depth from it.Basis:
each unit rate is drawn from a triangle between its minimum, likely and maximum in
borehole_cost_items.csv. The rates in one group of borehole_cost_spread.yaml share a cause and are
drawn together; the groups are drawn independently, so a cause shared across groups, such as the
exchange rate, is spread less than it should be. The minimum and maximum are provisional choices,
