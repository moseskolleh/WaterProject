no aquifer hasThe Bourdet derivative of the first step's drawdown, ds/d ln t with neighbours at
least 0.2 of a log cycle apart, is read from the slope of its log-log curve: a unit slope is casing
storage, a plateau radial flow, a half slope linear flow along a fracture, and a fall or a rise
after the plateau a boundary. On this test no run of three derivative points holds one slope over
enough of a log cycle to name a regime.The derivative's log-log slope names a regime as follows:
0.75 to 1.25 casing storage, 0.35 to 0.65 linear flow, within 0.15 of zero radial flow, and -0.25 or
less a falling derivative; a run must hold for three points and 0.3 of a log cycle. These limits are
judgements, set in the configuration. A derivative is a difference of readings and carries their
scatter: on synthetic radial-flow tests with 1 to 2 cm reading errors, a boundary was named in about
one test in a hundred, and where the derivative was under about 0.1 m some regime other than radial
flow was named in up to half. A named regime is a reason to look at the plot, not a finding on its
own.The Papadopulos-Cooper solution for a well of large diameter, fitted to all 10 readings of the
first step including those inside casing storage (casing radius 0.061 m; Laplace solution inverted
by Stehfest's method), gives T = 0.214 m2/day with an RMSE of 0.858 m. Its storativity is not given:
in the pumped well it trades off against the well radius, as the Theis fit's does, and on a poor fit
the readings barely constrain it. It is not adopted: its storativity of 0.53 is above 0.1, which no
aquifer has.Not adopted for the yield: Theis recovery (the recovery line meets t/t' = 1 at 21.7 m of
