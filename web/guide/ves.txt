Beside the suitability score, each point is given the chance that a borehole there yields enough for
a handpump through the dry season: a prior for the ground under it, from published yields,
multiplied by a factor for each thing the survey found. The table under each point shows how far
each factor moved it. Every figure here is provisional. Success means a yield of at least 1 m3/h
(0.28 L/s) at the dry-season water level, the handpump design rate in the configuration. Basis: the
prior from success_prior.csv and the likelihood ratios from success_evidence.yaml; calibration: the
toolkit's provisional default from PLAN.md step 3.3, adopted by no named person and not calibrated
against drilled outcomes. The band is the 10th to 90th percentile of a Beta prior with the table's
effective sample size, carried through the same factors; the factors themselves are provisional
choices. The suitability score is kept beside these odds for comparison. A (1): About 96 percent
(between 90 and over 99) that a borehole here yields enough for a handpump through the dry season.

The value of one more measurement Before the rig is called, one more sounding beside the
first-ranked point, or a profiling line through it, can change where to drill. Each is worth what it
is expected to save that decision, over everything it might read: the expected cost of the best
choice now, less the expected cost of the best choice once its reading is known. A second sounding
beside A (1) is worth up to US$ 11 to this decision and costs about US$ 100. It costs more than it
is worth to this decision. Without it, going to point B (2) is the cheaper choice: the chance of a
working borehole there is about 97 percent, against 96 at A (1). Going there straight away is
expected to cost US$ 7,790 for a working borehole; drilling at A (1) first, and going to point B (2)
if it is dry, US$ 7,794. The choice changes, to drilling at A (1), on the readings whose factors
multiply to more than 1.03, which the measurement reads with a chance of about 56 percent. Taking
the cheaper choice on each reading, the expected cost falls from US$ 7,790 to US$ 7,779. Knowing for
certain whether a borehole at A (1) would work would be worth US$ 121 to this decision; no
measurement can be worth more. The range of models has not been sampled at A (1), so a second
sounding is read, as this one was, for its water-zone resistivity alone, and is worth what a
profiling line is; sample the range to count its depth to basement too. Chance (percent) Chance at A

if it does not. Both places are taken to cost the same to drill. What a measurement could read are
the bands of success_evidence.yaml, its likelihood ratios raised to the weight of the point's fit
(0.6). The ratios do not say how often each band is seen, so the bands are spread among dry
boreholes as evenly as the ratios allow, and among working ones by the ratios; other spreads the
ratios allow give other values, larger or smaller, so the even spread is a choice and not a bound.

a borehole at the point would work; a sounding beside the first reads much of the same ground, so
this overstates what it adds. The cost of each measurement is a provisional figure in field.yaml
